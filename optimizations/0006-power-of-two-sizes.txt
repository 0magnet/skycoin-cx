0006 Power of two sizes for one instruction bound checks
Status: decided by Synth, D146 (all sizes powers of two, the stack size limit
a power of two). Not built. Open detail: array lengths, see below.

Problem: a bound check written as two compares (below zero, at or above the
end) costs two compares and two branches.

Change: the stack size limit, frame sizes and buffer sizes are powers of two.
The same rule covers the internal, heavily used structures of the runtime:
the call stack kept for stack traces, and the buffers of the built in IR
interpreter (vm) and the image runner (Synth, messages 778 and 779).
A check against a power of two size 2^k is one AND with the mask
NOT(2^k - 1) and one jump if the result is not zero:
  stack:  (fp + frame) AND NOT(2^20 - 1), jump to stop if not zero
  index:  index AND NOT(2^k - 1), jump to stop if not zero
A negative index has its high bits set, so the same test catches it.
On x86 the test and the jump fuse into one micro-op (test + jnz). On ARM it
is tst + b.ne.

Open detail: an array declared with a length that is not a power of two,
for example [100]. Either the length must be a power of two (compile error
otherwise), or the storage is rounded up to 128 and the mask check tests
against 128, which misses indexes 100 to 127. 0007 covers any length exactly
with one unsigned compare.

The compiler writes a comment next to each check in the emitted IR, naming
the check and its mask.
